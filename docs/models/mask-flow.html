Step 1: the mask model (flow matching, 26 features)

Turns 26 clinical features, the slice position and the heart phase, plus random noise, into a synthetic segmentation mask of the heart. This is the final mask generator.

Models: Mask model (step 1, final) · Mask model, first version (VAE) · Flow model (step 2) · SPADE GAN (baseline)

What it does

A mask is a map of the heart: every pixel is background, right ventricle (RV), myocardium or left ventricle (LV). Given a clinical profile, the model draws such a map. A different noise draw gives a different heart for the same profile. It replaced the first version, a conditional VAE with 14 features (page), because the VAE masks were too regular and a size sweep hardly changed them.

Input data

  • Real data: the 85 training patients of ACDC, end-diastole (ED) and end-systole (ES) slices, cropped to 128 × 128 pixels at 1.5 mm.
  • Condition: the 26 features calculated from the masks and the patient record (see the feature table), plus the relative slice position and an ED/ES flag. Skewed features are log-transformed and all are standardised with the training patients only.
  • Split: 85 training and 15 validation patients, split by patient. The ACDC test set and M&Ms are not used yet.

How it works

  • A mask is written as four channels with values ±1. Flow matching trains a network to predict the velocity that carries random noise to the real mask.
  • The network is a U-Net with 6.4 million parameters. The 26 features, the slice position and the ED/ES flag scale and shift every block (FiLM).
  • During training the features are dropped at random (classifier-free guidance). At generation the guidance weight is 1.
  • An exponential moving average of the weights is used, and the loss gives foreground pixels a weight of 1. A weight of 3 made the RV and myocardium too large, so it was lowered.
  • Generation takes 50 Euler steps from noise. The most likely channel per pixel gives the label map.
  • An anatomy filter checks the structures. A failed mask is redrawn, up to 20 times.

Visual results

Results

6 runs (2 training seeds × 3 noise seeds) on the 15 validation patients, 254 slices.

  • Anatomy: 68% of first-draw masks pass (63 to 75% across runs), 99.9% after the filter. Slice-area correlation with the real masks 0.82 / 0.87 / 0.90 for RV, myocardium and LV.
  • Follows the requested features: mean correlation 0.79 over the features, 45% of values within 10%. LV ejection fraction r 0.92 with an error of 7.6 points.
  • Shuffle control: the masks follow the features they are given (r 0.76) and not the patient’s own (r −0.17).
  • Diagnosis read back from the masks: 89%, against 93% on real masks. One patient is worth 6.7 points.

More figures and every feature are in the full technical details.

Limits

  • Only 68% of first draws pass the anatomy checks, so the filter is needed.
  • Circularity is poorly reproduced (r 0.32 to 0.63), as are septal thickness at ES, the maximum wall thickness at ES, the RV ejection fraction (r 0.55) and very small hearts.
  • Trained on ACDC only, evaluated on 15 validation patients. Memorisation of this model has not been tested yet.
