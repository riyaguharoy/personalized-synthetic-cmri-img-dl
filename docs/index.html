Clinical-Profile-Conditioned Synthetic Cardiac MRI Generation Using Deep Generative Models

Can a patient's clinical profile steer synthetic cardiac MRI?

Clinical features and noise make a heart mask. The mask makes the image. This page shows where the project stands.

ResultsAbstractFull technical details

Abstract

Deep learning for cardiac MRI needs a lot of labelled data, but scans are scarce, differ between scanners and are protected by privacy rules. Generative models can add synthetic scans, but most only control the anatomy of the heart, for example with a mask. This project asks whether generation can be controlled by information about the patient: height, weight, chamber volumes, ejection fractions and wall measures.

A two-stage pipeline turns 26 clinical features and noise into a segmentation mask, and the mask and noise into a synthetic cardiac MR image. A SPADE GAN baseline is compared with flow matching. The result is judged on image quality, on whether the masks follow the profile they were given, and (still to do) on usefulness and privacy.

System architecture

Why: Two stages let each part be tested alone: an effect of the features can be traced to the stage that produced it, and the image model can be tested with real masks. Flow matching is the one model family used in both stages, since the thesis studies the effect of the clinical profile and not which model family is best.

Stage 1: features to mask
  • Flow matching, FiLM U-Net, 6.4 M parameters
  • 26 features, slice position and ED/ES flag as the condition
  • Classifier-free guidance, EMA, 50 Euler steps
  • Anatomy filter redraws failed masks (up to 20 times)
Stage 2: mask to image
  • Flow matching velocity U-Net, 24.7 M parameters
  • Mask added in every block, guidance scale 2, 50 steps
  • Baseline: mask-conditioned SPADE GAN

The two stages are built and tested alone, so an effect of the features can be traced to the stage that produced it. Diagrams of each model are in Models.

The 26 clinical features

Why: Each feature can be calculated from a mask, so it can be measured again on a generated mask and checked against the value it was given. The first 14 carry diagnosis information, the 12 shape features were added because the first masks were too regular, and all 26 were kept because dropping circularity lowered the diagnosis read-back from 89% to 78%.

The first 14 are the features used to classify the five ACDC diagnoses (Wolterink et al.). The 12 shape features (rows 15 to 26) were added later. Every feature except height and weight is calculated from a segmentation mask, so it can be measured again on a generated mask. Skewed features are log-transformed and all are standardised before they enter the model.

Pipeline

Why: The mask sits between the stages because the features are measured on masks, so the conditioning can be checked before any image exists. The experiments go from real masks to generated masks to separate the errors of the image model from those of the mask generator.

1. Features26 measures of the heart (volumes, ejection fractions, wall thickness, shape), height and weight.
2. MaskThe mask generator draws a segmentation mask from the features and noise.
3. ImageThe image model turns the mask into a synthetic cardiac MR slice.

Models used

Why: The models are listed in the order they were built. Flow matching replaced the VAE for masks because the VAE masks were too regular, and replaced the GAN for images because its FID was much lower (118.8 against 189.5).

Conditional VAE, the first mask model (stage 1). 14 features, slice position and ED/ES flag. An encoder squeezes the real mask and the condition into 16 numbers. At generation only the decoder is used, with random noise.

Why: A conditional VAE is the usual way to generate shapes from patient information in related work (Beetz, CHeart, 4D CardioSynth), so it was the natural first model.

  • 2.2 M parameters
  • Loss: per-pixel classification with extra weight on the LV, plus a KL term
  • Anatomy pass 53.8%. Masks are smooth and too regular, and a size sweep hardly changes them

Flow matching mask generator, final (stage 1). 26 features, slice position and ED/ES flag scale and shift every block of a U-Net (FiLM). Noise is carried to a mask over 50 Euler steps.

Why: Its masks vary more and scale with the requested size, and the images made from them are as good as images from real masks. The VAE followed the feature values more closely, but its masks were too regular.

  • 6.4 M parameters, classifier-free guidance (weight 1), EMA weights
  • Anatomy filter redraws failed masks, up to 20 times
  • 68% pass on the first draw, 99.9% after the filter. LV ejection fraction r 0.92. Diagnosis read-back 89%

SPADE GAN, the baseline (stage 2). The generator starts from 128 random numbers. Residual blocks with SPADE layers re-scale the features of each block using the mask and up-sample to 128 × 128 pixels. It is trained against a discriminator.

Why: It is the baseline from the proposal, a mask-conditioned GAN, and the SPADE layers keep the anatomy where the mask puts it.

  • About 14.6 M parameters in the generator, 2.8 M in the discriminator
  • FID 189.5 and KID 0.122 with real masks (lower is better)
  • Noisy images and little variety, kept as the baseline

Flow matching image model (stage 2). A velocity U-Net with the mask added in every block. The mask is hidden in 10% of training steps, so guidance can be used at generation (scale 2, 50 steps).

Why: It reaches a far lower FID than the GAN and makes sharper, more varied images. The mask is hidden in 10% of training steps so that guidance can be used at generation.

  • 24.7 M parameters, EMA weights
  • FID 118.8 and KID 0.040 with real masks, against a floor of 99.0 for real against real images
  • Sharper and more varied images than the GAN

Glossary

Why: These terms are used in the pipeline, models and results above. A paper is cited where a method, metric or dataset comes from one, so each claim can be traced to its source.

TermMeaning and sourceWhy it is used here
ACDCAutomated Cardiac Diagnosis Challenge dataset. Bernard et al., Deep learning techniques for automatic MRI cardiac multi-structures segmentation and diagnosis: Is the problem solved? IEEE Transactions on Medical Imaging 37(11), 2018.Public data with expert masks, diagnosis, height and weight, which the features and the diagnosis read-back need.
M&MsMulti-centre, multi-vendor cardiac segmentation dataset. Campello et al., Multi-centre, multi-vendor and multi-disease cardiac segmentation: The M&Ms challenge. IEEE Transactions on Medical Imaging 40(12), 2021.Adds other scanners and centres for an external test.
Segmentation maskA map that labels every pixel as background, right ventricle (RV), myocardium (the heart muscle) or left ventricle (LV).It links the two stages and lets every feature be measured again on generated output.
ED / ESEnd-diastole (heart full) and end-systole (heart squeezed), the two phases used.Volumes and ejection fraction need both phases.
Ejection fraction (EF)The share of the blood pumped out in one beat, 100 × (ED volume − ES volume) ÷ ED volume.A key measure of heart function and a feature checked on generated masks.
Classification featuresThe 14 features used to classify the ACDC diagnoses. Wolterink et al., Automatic Segmentation and Disease Classification Using Cardiac Cine MR Images, arXiv 1708.01141.The starting feature set, because they carry diagnosis information.
Conditional VAEEncoder and decoder with the condition as input, new samples come from random latent numbers. Kingma and Welling, Auto-encoding variational Bayes. ICLR 2014.The first mask model, a common approach in related work.
Flow matchingTrains a network to predict the velocity that carries noise to data along simple paths. Lipman et al., Flow matching for generative modeling. ICLR 2023.Used in both stages and gave more varied masks than the VAE.
FiLMFeature-wise scaling and shifting of a network layer from a condition. Perez et al., FiLM: Visual reasoning with a general conditioning layer. AAAI 2018.Lets the features steer every block of the mask network.
Classifier-free guidanceDrops the condition at random in training and mixes the conditioned and unconditioned prediction at generation. Ho and Salimans, Classifier-free diffusion guidance. NeurIPS Workshop 2021.Lets the strength of the condition be set at generation.
SPADESpatially-adaptive normalisation that feeds a mask into every layer of a generator. Park et al., Semantic image synthesis with spatially-adaptive normalization. CVPR 2019.Keeps anatomy where the mask puts it in the baseline GAN.
U-NetEncoder-decoder network with skip connections. Ronneberger et al., U-Net: Convolutional networks for biomedical image segmentation. MICCAI 2015.The backbone of both flow models.
FIDFréchet Inception Distance, distance between real and generated image statistics, lower is better. Heusel et al., GANs trained by a two time-scale update rule converge to a local Nash equilibrium. NeurIPS 2017.The standard fidelity measure, read against the real-versus-real floor of 99.0.
KIDKernel Inception Distance, less biased than FID on few images. Bińkowski et al., Demystifying MMD GANs. ICLR 2018.Added because it is less biased than FID with few images (254 validation slices).
Dice scoreOverlap of two masks, 1 is perfect. Dice, Measures of the amount of ecologic association between species. Ecology 26(3), 1945.Planned score for the usefulness test with segmentation models.
Anatomy filterA check on the structures of a generated mask. A failed mask is redrawn, up to 20 times.Needed because only 68% of first-draw masks pass the checks.
Diagnosis read-backA diagnosis classifier is applied to generated masks. Its accuracy on real masks (93%) is the ceiling.Checks that the masks carry the diagnosis of the requested features, not only the numbers.
