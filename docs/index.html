Clinical-Profile-Conditioned Synthetic Cardiac MRI Generation Using Deep Generative Models

Can a patient's clinical profile steer a generator of synthetic cardiac MRI? Clinical features and noise make a heart mask, and the mask makes the image. This page shows the current state of the project.

Research Abstract

Deep learning for cardiac MRI needs a lot of labelled data, but scans are scarce, differ between scanners and are protected by privacy rules. Generative models can add synthetic scans, but most only control the anatomy of the heart, for example with a mask. This project asks whether generation can be controlled by information about the patient: height, weight, chamber volumes, ejection fractions and wall measures.

We use a two-stage pipeline. Stage 1 turns 26 clinical features and noise into a segmentation mask. Stage 2 turns the mask and noise into a synthetic cardiac MR image, comparing a SPADE GAN baseline with a flow matching model. The generator is judged on image quality, on whether the masks follow the profile they were given, and (still to do) on usefulness and privacy.

Objectives

Main objective. Investigate how clinical-profile information can give fine-grained control over the generation of synthetic cardiac MR images together with their segmentation masks.

O1. Build it

Develop a baseline generative model without clinical-profile conditioning, and a conditioned version built on top of it.

O2. Check it works

Check that the generated images and masks keep plausible anatomy and reflect the profile they were conditioned on.

O3. Compare it

Compare the conditioned model with the baseline on fidelity, downstream utility and privacy.

Clinical features

The 26 clinical featuresequations and worked examples

Why these 26

  • The first 14 are the features used to classify the five ACDC diagnoses in the challenge paper by Wolterink et al.: height, weight, volumes, ejection fractions and ratios. They carry the diagnosis information.
  • The 12 added (wall thickness, septal thickness, circularity) were picked from a list of 57 features in the literature, after the first masks were judged too regular. They describe wall and shape, which the first 14 only cover indirectly (for example, a thick wall in HCM or a rounder, dilated LV in DCM).
  • Rule for every feature: it must be calculable from a mask alone, so it can be measured again on a generated mask and the mask checked against the value it was given.
  • Test: groups of features were removed one at a time. Septal thickness and wall-thickness spread helped their own control, and circularity carried some diagnosis signal (78% read-back without it, 89% with it). Mean and maximum thickness gave no clear gain but did no harm, so all 26 were kept.

14 features were used first and 12 (wall thickness and shape, rows 15 to 26) were added on 3 to 5 November. All except height and weight are calculated from the segmentation mask, so they can be measured on generated masks in exactly the same way. All examples use round numbers, not a real patient.

Step 1: from a mask to a volume

A mask labels every pixel as background, right ventricle (RV), myocardium (MYO) or left ventricle (LV). A volume is the number of pixels with a label, summed over all slices of a phase, times the volume of one voxel. ED is end-diastole (heart full), ES is end-systole (heart squeezed).

Vkph = Nkph × Δx × Δy × Δz ÷ 1000

N is the pixel count, Δx = Δy = 1.5 mm, Δz is the slice thickness (for example 10 mm), and dividing by 1000 turns mm³ into mL.

Example. 5,000 LV pixels at ED and 10 mm slices: one voxel is 1.5 × 1.5 × 10 = 22.5 mm³, so VLVED = 5,000 × 22.5 ÷ 1000 = 112.5 mL. With 2,000 LV pixels at ES: VLVES = 45 mL.

Step 2: ejection fractions and ratios

Example. EFLV = 100 × (112.5 − 45) ÷ 112.5 = 60%. If the RV volume at ED is 100 mL, the RV/LV ratio at ED is 100 ÷ 112.5 = 0.89. If the myocardium volume at ED is 90 mL, the myocardium/LV ratio is 90 ÷ 112.5 = 0.80.

Step 3: wall thickness

On each slice the myocardium is thinned to its centre line. For each centre-line pixel, thickness is t = 2 × d × 1.5 mm, where d is the distance in pixels to the nearest edge of the myocardium. All centre-line values of all slices in a phase are pooled, then the mean, standard deviation and maximum are taken. Septal thickness uses only the centre-line pixels within 4 pixels of the RV.

Example. A centre-line pixel with d = 4 pixels has t = 2 × 4 × 1.5 = 12 mm. If the pooled values were 10, 12 and 14 mm, the mean is 12 mm, the spread (SD) is √((4 + 0 + 4) ÷ 3) = 1.6 mm and the maximum is 14 mm.

Step 4: circularity

For the largest region of the LV (or RV) on a slice, C = 4π × area ÷ perimeter². A perfect circle gives 1, and elongated or irregular shapes give less. The value is averaged over the slices where the structure is present.

Example. A circle of radius 20 pixels has area π × 20² = 1,257 and perimeter 2π × 20 = 125.7, so C = 4π × 1,257 ÷ 125.7² = 1.00. An irregular region with area 1,000 and perimeter 140 gives 4π × 1,000 ÷ 19,600 = 0.64.

Generated Output

5.1 Features to masks

Masks generated from clinical features for patients the model never saw. Colours: blue right ventricle, olive myocardium, red left ventricle.

5.2 Generated masks to synthetic CMR images

One unseen validation patient per diagnosis group. From top to bottom: the real image, the real mask, the image the model makes from the real mask, the mask generated from the 26 clinical features and noise (final generator, with the anatomy filter), and the image made from that generated mask.

The generated masks have the size and shape expected for each group (large LV in DCM, small heart in HCM), and the image model turns them into plausible cardiac scans. The generated masks are not copies of the real ones: the RV is often larger than the real RV, and the very small HCM heart at ES is generated with a larger LV cavity than the real one.

Results

FINDING 1

Flow matching is much better

FID falls from 189.5 to 118.8: about 20 points above the real-vs-real floor, against about 90 for the GAN.

FINDING 2

The full generator works

From features and noise alone, with no real mask or image, images score as well as with real masks.

FINDING 3

The features steer the masks

Masks follow the features they are given (correlation 0.76) and not the patient's own (−0.17).

FINDING 4

Precision varies by feature

Strong for size and volumes, moderate for ejection fraction, weak for fine wall details.

Reading. LV and myocardium volumes, the RV/LV and myocardium/LV ratios and mean wall thickness are followed well (r 0.88 to 0.99); RV volumes a little less (r 0.85 to 0.89, RV at ES slope 0.70). Ejection fractions and the end-systolic RV/LV ratio are compressed (slopes 0.35 to 0.71): the group means show DCM and HCM EF pulled towards the middle, and MINF (requested 21%) generated at 35%. Wall-thickness spread, septal thickness at ES and circularity are only loosely followed (r 0.3 to 0.7). Shuffle control: every feature follows the fed value more than the own value, but septal ES (0.22) and LV circularity at ED (0.37) only weakly.

Limits. 15 validation patients, 2 training seeds, and the model choice was made on the same patients. Usefulness for training and privacy (membership inference) are not tested yet.

Glossary terms

Heart and data

TermMeaning
CMRCardiac magnetic resonance: a scan that films the beating heart without radiation.
LV, RV, myocardiumLeft and right ventricle (the two pumping chambers) and the muscle ring around the LV.
ED, ESEnd-diastole (heart full) and end-systole (heart squeezed). Both phases are used.
EFEjection fraction: 100 × (ED volume − ES volume) ÷ ED volume, the share of blood pumped out per beat.
Segmentation maskA label image: every pixel is background, RV, myocardium or LV.
NOR, DCM, HCM, MINF, RVThe five ACDC groups: normal, dilated cardiomyopathy, hypertrophic cardiomyopathy, previous heart attack, abnormal right ventricle.
Clinical profileThe patient information used as the condition: here the 26 features.

Methods and evaluation

TermMeaning
ConditioningGiving a model extra information (the profile or a mask) so the output follows it.
VAEA model that compresses and rebuilds data through a small random code. First stage 1 model.
GAN, SPADEA generator trained against a discriminator. SPADE feeds the mask into every generator block so the image keeps its shape.
Flow matchingTurns noise into data in many small steps along a learned direction (50 steps here).
Classifier-free guidanceTrain with the condition sometimes dropped, then push the output towards the condition when generating.
FiLMA layer that scales and shifts features using the condition.
FID, KIDDistances between real and synthetic image sets, from Inception features. Lower is better. The real-vs-real floor (FID 99.0) is always shown for reference.
Anatomy checks, filterRules a mask must pass (one region per class, LV present, myocardium wraps the LV, RV touches the heart). The filter redraws a failing mask.
Shuffle controlFeed one patient the features of another and see which one the mask follows.
Round tripMeasure the features of a generated mask and compare them with the requested ones.
